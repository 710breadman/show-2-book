# Research and Market Viability

Updated 20 June 2026. This is practical research, not legal advice.

## Editorial and length conclusion

The project's core audience is the preschool/read-aloud bracket. Published Bluey hardbacks commonly use 32 pages; Penguin lists *Bluey: Perfect* as a 32-page book for ages 3-5, and its episode-based *Fairytale* hardback is also 32 pages. Children's publishing guidance generally places a commercial picture book for ages 4-8 at or below about 500 words, while broader guidance commonly allows 300-800 words. Because this project is a parent-led episode retelling rather than a submission manuscript, a practical target is:

- seven-minute episode: 500-800 words over 18-22 story pages;
- visually driven episode: 250-500 words with quieter page turns;
- extended special: scale by meaningful beats rather than runtime alone.

The current *Magic Xylophone* page count is appropriate for a digital/home read-aloud edition. A commercial printer edition would need deliberate front/back matter or paired spreads to reach a supported 24- or 32-page interior; filler should never be added to the story merely to satisfy a printer.

Sources: [Penguin - Bluey: Perfect](https://www.penguin.com.au/books/bluey-perfect-9781761046292), [Penguin - Bluey: Fairytale](https://www.penguin.com.au/books/bluey-fairytale-9781761344787), [Boyds Mills picture-book word-count guidance](https://boydsmills.org/blog/picture-book-word-count-and-other-rules-meant-to-be-broken/).

## What *The Magic Xylophone* is really about

The official episode page centers the conflict on Bluey's reluctance to give up control, Bingo's repeated attempts to be included, and the sisters' eventual cooperation. Community discussions repeatedly remember Bandit's total commitment to the game and the Daddy water-fountain ending, but the emotional hinge is Bingo finally saying that Bluey's behavior makes her sad.

The ending therefore should not sound like a classroom rule about equal turns. The more faithful idea is that a game becomes better when everyone gets to belong in it. Recommended final line:

> The best games aren't about getting every turn. They're about making sure everyone gets to be part of the fun.

Sources: [official episode page](https://www.bluey.tv/watch/season-1/the-magic-xylophone/), [IMDb plot summary](https://www.imdb.com/title/tt8865002/plotsummary/), [r/bluey episode discussion](https://www.reddit.com/r/bluey/comments/16wze16/rate_the_episode_magic_xylophone_s1e1/), [r/bluey community reflections](https://www.reddit.com/r/bluey/comments/1n7lihx/day_1_what_are_your_thoughts_on_the_magic/).

## Commercial viability: Bluey-branded books

Selling these books is not responsibly viable without written permission/licensing.

The project reproduces frames from a copyrighted television episode, uses protected characters, and prepares a derivative story adaptation. The U.S. Copyright Office identifies reproduction, preparation of derivative works, public distribution, and public display as exclusive rights of the copyright owner. The official Bluey site states that BLUEY and the character logo are owned by Ludo Studio and licensed by BBC Studios Distribution Ltd. Facebook's commerce policy also says listings may not violate third-party copyright or trademark rights.

Noncommercial private family use reduces practical exposure but is not an automatic fair-use guarantee. The work is highly creative, uses many important frames and spoken lines, and could overlap with the market for licensed Bluey storybooks. Commercial sale makes the fair-use position substantially weaker. Do not list this version on Facebook Marketplace, Etsy, KDP, or another storefront.

Sources: [U.S. Copyright Office - exclusive rights](https://www.copyright.gov/what-is-copyright/), [U.S. Copyright Office - fair-use factors](https://www.copyright.gov/fair-use/), [Meta Commerce Policy - third-party infringement](https://www.facebook.com/policies_center/commerce/third_party_infringement), [official Bluey rights notice](https://www.bluey.tv/watch/season-1/the-magic-xylophone/).

## The two viable paths

### 1. Private family editions

Keep the files unlisted and make only the copies needed for the household. A print provider may still refuse copyrighted material under its own terms, so check before ordering. Do not describe a private print order as licensed or official.

### 2. A commercial original-character series

Reuse the production framework - episode dossier, emotional beat sheet, read-aloud editing, frame logic, typography, and final-message discipline - with characters, art, dialogue, and stories you own. This path is commercially viable with almost no startup capital:

1. Produce one polished 24- or 32-page pilot with original art.
2. Read it to 5-10 families and record where children interrupt, laugh, drift, or ask for another reading.
3. Revise to a 5-8 minute read-aloud.
4. Order one proof; photograph the physical result.
5. Use preorders or crowdfunding before any inventory purchase.
6. Launch print-on-demand first, then move to a short run only after real demand exists.

A formal Bluey licensing inquiry can be made through BBC Studios' brands/licensing channels, but major preschool brands normally select established licensees with distribution capacity. With little startup capital, this should be treated as a long-shot future route, not the operating plan. Sources: [BBC Studios ANZ brands and licensing](https://www.bbcstudios.com.au/brands), [BBC Studios Bluey licensing program](https://www.bbcstudiospressroom.com/press/bbc-studios-expands-licensing-program-for-bluey-with-five-new-partners/).

## Printing options and current price bands

Prices below are observed or calculated in USD before tax and shipping. Always re-quote at order time.

### $0-$10: screen-first or home prototype

- Read from the PDF on a tablet/TV: no incremental cost.
- Home duplex prototype: paper, ink, and a simple report cover or binder. Real cost varies heavily with ink coverage; episode screenshots are ink-intensive.

### About $17-$30: local copy-shop prototype

Staples currently shows custom color copies starting around $0.80 per page. A 21-page single-sided color prototype is therefore about $16.80 before heavier paper, cover stock, cutting, lamination, or binding. A practical bound for one finished copy is roughly $20-$35. [Staples color copies](https://www.staples.com/color-copies/product_1798666).

### About $24-$46: one gift-quality landscape book

Blurb lists 10 x 8 inch landscape photo books at $24 paperback or $41 image-wrap hardcover on standard paper; premium paper is $29 paperback or $46 hardcover. It permits single-copy orders. This is the strongest one-off family-copy option if the provider accepts the content. [Blurb pricing](https://www.blurb.com/pricing).

### About $2.92 manufacturing cost: print-on-demand paperback, original/licensed content only

KDP premium color accepts a minimum of 24 pages. Large trim pricing is currently $1.00 fixed plus $0.080 per page, making a 24-page large-trim paperback about $2.92 to manufacture before Amazon's retail margin. Premium color is specifically recommended for image-heavy children's books. KDP is not a lawful workaround for unlicensed Bluey content. [KDP color ink options](https://kdp.amazon.com/help/topic/GX56BFPW4BKNPGFW).

### Short run and offset

- 10-49 copies: print-on-demand volume discounts or a digital short-run printer; expect a live quote rather than a reliable universal per-unit price.
- 50-99 copies: Blurb lists a 25% volume discount for photo books and paperback/hardcover books.
- 100+ copies: request short-run quotes after preorders.
- 500+ copies: offset can lower per-unit cost, but freight, proofs, storage, damaged inventory, and a large upfront payment make it inappropriate without demonstrated demand.

## Recommendation

Finish this Bluey series as a private family reading library. Do not market or sell it. If the format proves delightful in repeated read-alouds, use the framework to develop an original-character series and validate demand with one proof and preorders before spending meaningful money.
