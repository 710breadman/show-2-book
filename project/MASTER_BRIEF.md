# Bluey Episode Picture-Book Project - Refined Master Brief

## Purpose

Create warm, highly polished picture-book retellings of selected *Bluey* episodes for private family read-aloud use. Preserve the comedy, emotional intelligence, rhythm, and understatement that make each episode work. The result should feel thoughtfully edited and visually cohesive, never like a transcript pasted beneath screenshots.

## Non-negotiable editorial rules

1. The embedded English subtitle track is the dialogue authority. Spoken lines should remain as close to the episode as clarity and page rhythm allow.
2. Use natural contractions whenever the characters use them: *it's, you're, don't, won't, I'll, she's, he's, can't*.
3. Never invent a quotation that sounds like episode dialogue. New language belongs in narration.
4. Identify speakers naturally: “said Bingo,” “called Bluey,” “Dad whispered,” followed by a visible action when useful.
5. Narration bridges cuts and explains only what a child cannot infer from the image. It should not explain the joke, flatten subtext, or announce the lesson inside the story.
6. Preserve childlike grammar when it is character-revealing and subtitle-authentic, such as Bingo's “you always never take turns with me.”
7. Every image must match the adjacent beat. Select frames by exact timestamp, not merely by nearby scene.
8. Sound effects may sit on the art only when they are audible or visually implied. Place them beside their source and never over a face or important gesture.
9. Use a two-frame or three-frame comic sequence only when one still cannot communicate an essential change of action.
10. End each book with one short, beautiful “something to remember” line. Research the episode first; do not turn subtle episodes into lectures.

## Child and read-aloud target

- Primary audience: ages 3-6, read aloud with an adult.
- Standard seven-minute episode: 18-22 story pages, roughly 500-800 words, about 5-8 minutes aloud.
- Quiet or visually driven episodes may use fewer words and more image-led pages.
- Extended episodes scale with runtime and narrative complexity. *The Sign* should be substantially longer: approximately 40-56 story pages or a deliberately divided two-part edition, not a seven-minute template stretched thin.
- Typical story page: 20-45 words. Emotional hinge pages may reach about 60 words if the layout remains easy to read.
- Reading copy and print interior are separate outputs: the reading copy may use the natural page count; a printer-ready edition should be padded deliberately to a supported signature or platform minimum, commonly 24 or 32 pages.

## Visual system

- Landscape 11 x 8.5 inch reading edition; preserve a print-safe margin and optional bleed-aware variant.
- Full-width 16:9 episode image with a light pastel frame and a generous text panel.
- Body text: highly legible rounded humanist sans serif. Dialogue: playful rounded display face. Avoid an imitation of the protected *Bluey* logo.
- Character dialogue colors remain consistent across the whole series.
- Effects use high contrast, a restrained outline, and a playful tilt. Effects must be anchored to the correct character/object.
- Source frames are extracted at the exact beat, then enlarged with high-quality Lanczos resampling and gentle sharpening. No generative repainting of characters: fidelity matters more than invented detail.
- Use consistent alt text and embedded fonts in the final PDF.

## Episode research dossier required before scripting

For every episode, record:

- verified local file and runtime;
- extracted subtitle transcript;
- official *Bluey* synopsis and episode details;
- IMDb synopsis/reviews as a secondary check;
- at least one substantial r/bluey community discussion;
- a wiki or detailed recap for beat-level cross-checking;
- the episode's surface story, emotional hinge, adult subtext, favourite community moments, and final-message hypothesis;
- scenes that must not be over-explained;
- a beat sheet with subtitle timestamps and candidate frame timestamps.

## Production gates

1. Verify the title from subtitles rather than trusting the filename number.
2. Lock the episode dossier and emotional thesis.
3. Draft the beat sheet before selecting final frames.
4. Draft dialogue from subtitles, then add minimal narration.
5. Run a contraction and quotation audit.
6. Review frame/text agreement page by page.
7. Render the full PDF and visually inspect every page at 100%.
8. Audit word count, read-aloud time, font size, image resolution, page order, alt text, and final-message restraint.
